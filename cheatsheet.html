Chapter 6 — Front Joint · Independence · Sums · Conditionals

Joint (§6.1)

Joint CDF
$$F(x,y)=P(X\le x,\,Y\le y)$$
  • $F(-\infty,y)=F(x,-\infty)=0$
  • $F(\infty,\infty)=1$
  • $P(a_1{<}X{\le}a_2,\,b_1{<}Y{\le}b_2)=F(a_2,b_2){-}F(a_1,b_2){-}F(a_2,b_1){+}F(a_1,b_1)$
Joint PMF (discrete)
$$p(x,y)=P(X=x,\,Y=y)$$ Marginals: $p_X(x)=\sum_y p(x,y)$
Joint PDF (continuous)
$$P((X,Y)\in A)=\iint_A f(x,y)\,dx\,dy$$ Marginals: $f_X(x)=\int f(x,y)\,dy$
$f(x,y)=\dfrac{\partial^2 F}{\partial x\,\partial y}$

Independence (§6.2)

Factorization
$X\perp Y \iff F(x,y)=F_X(x)F_Y(y)$
Equivalently: $f(x,y)=f_X(x)f_Y(y)$
Lemma: If $f(x,y)=g(x)h(y)$ on a rectangular support, then indep.
Trap
Support NOT rectangular ⇒ NOT independent, even if $f$ factors.
Example: $f(x,y)=2$ on $0<x<y<1$ — looks like $2\cdot 1$ but $x<y$ couples them.
Consequences
  • $E[XY]=E[X]E[Y]$
  • $\mathrm{Var}(X{+}Y)=\mathrm{Var}(X){+}\mathrm{Var}(Y)$
  • $M_{X+Y}(t)=M_X(t)M_Y(t)$
Sketching the Support
Draw boundary lines (equalities), pick a test point, shade region.
{0<x<y<1}: unit square below line $y=x$ — triangle above diagonal.
{x+y<1, x,y>0}: triangle with vertices $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$.
{$|x|$<y, y>0}: wedge between lines $y=x$ and $y=-x$.
{$x^2+y^2$<$r^2$}: disk of radius $r$ (use polar).
Non-rectangular support ⇒ NOT independent (even if $f$ factors).
Setting Up Double Integral Bounds
Sketch → fix outer variable → read inner limits as functions of outer.
Region$dy$ inner$dx$ inner
$0<x<y<1$$\int_0^1\!\int_x^1 f\,dy\,dx$$\int_0^1\!\int_0^y f\,dx\,dy$
$x+y<1$, $x,y>0$$\int_0^1\!\int_0^{1-x} f\,dy\,dx$$\int_0^1\!\int_0^{1-y} f\,dx\,dy$
Fubini: swap order freely when $f\ge 0$ and region is same.

Sums & Convolution (§6.3)

Convolution
Discrete: $p_{X+Y}(a)=\sum_k p_X(k)\,p_Y(a{-}k)$
Continuous: $f_{X+Y}(a)=\int f_X(x)\,f_Y(a{-}x)\,dx$
Integrate only where both densities are nonzero.

MGF Trick
If $X\perp Y$: $M_{X+Y}(t)=M_X(t)M_Y(t)$.
Recognize the product → identify the sum distribution.
Expectation/Variance
$E[aX+bY]=aE[X]+bE[Y]$ (always)
$\mathrm{Var}(aX{+}bY)=a^2\mathrm{Var}(X){+}b^2\mathrm{Var}(Y){+}2ab\,\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$
If indep: Cov = 0.
Covariance & Correlation
$\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=E[XY]-E[X]E[Y]$
$\mathrm{Cov}(X,X)=\mathrm{Var}(X)$; bilinear & symmetric
$\mathrm{Cov}(aX{+}b,\,cY{+}d)=ac\,\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$
$\mathrm{Cov}\!\left(\sum a_iX_i,\sum b_jY_j\right)=\sum_{i,j}a_ib_j\,\mathrm{Cov}(X_i,Y_j)$
$\rho=\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)/(\sigma_X\sigma_Y)\in[-1,1]$
Indep ⇒ Cov=0; converse FALSE.
Sum of Normals — Lecture 6D
If $X_k\sim N(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)$ are independent: $$\sum_{k=1}^n X_k\sim N\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^n\mu_k,\;\sum_{k=1}^n\sigma_k^2\right)$$ Linear combo: $aX+bY\sim N(a\mu_X+b\mu_Y,\;a^2\sigma_X^2+b^2\sigma_Y^2)$
Proof: convolution + complete the square, sub $w=x-\tfrac{at}{\sqrt{a+b}}$.
Gamma Sum from iid Exp — Lecture 6D
$X_1,\ldots,X_n$ iid $\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$, $Y_n=X_1+\cdots+X_n$: $$f_{Y_n}(t)=\frac{\lambda^n\,t^{n-1}}{\Gamma(n)}\,e^{-\lambda t},\quad t\ge 0$$ So $Y_n\sim\Gamma(n,\lambda)$. Proved by induction: each step convolves with $\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$, yielding $\lambda^k t^{k-1}/(k-1)!\ e^{-\lambda t}$.
Beta Integral & Gamma+Gamma Sum — 6D
$$\int_0^1 x^{a-1}(1{-}x)^{b-1}dx=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}\quad\text{(Beta integral)}$$ Scaled: $\displaystyle\int_0^t x^{\alpha-1}(t{-}x)^{\beta-1}dx=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}\,t^{\alpha+\beta-1}$
Used in convolution to prove $\Gamma(\gamma,\lambda)+\Gamma(\delta,\lambda)=\Gamma(\gamma+\delta,\lambda)$.
Beta$(a,b)$: $E[X]=\dfrac{a}{a+b}$, $\mathrm{Var}(X)=\dfrac{ab}{(a+b)^2(a+b+1)}$

Conditionals (§6.4–6.5)

Conditional PMF / PDF
$$p_{X|Y}(x|y)=\dfrac{p(x,y)}{p_Y(y)} \quad \text{(discrete)}$$ $$f_{X|Y}(x|y)=\dfrac{f(x,y)}{f_Y(y)} \quad \text{(continuous)}$$ For fixed $y$: $\sum_x p_{X\mid Y}=1$ or $\int f_{X\mid Y}\,dx=1$.
Conditional Expectation
$$E[X\mid Y=y]=\sum_x x\,p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)$$ $$E[X\mid Y=y]=\int x\,f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)\,dx$$ $E[X|Y]$ is a random variable (function of $Y$).
Law of Total Expectation
$$E[X]=E\big[E[X|Y]\big]$$ Discrete: $E[X]=\sum_y E[X|Y{=}y]\,p_Y(y)$
Cts: $E[X]=\int E[X|Y{=}y]\,f_Y(y)\,dy$
Law of Total Variance
$$\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[\mathrm{Var}(X|Y)]+\mathrm{Var}(E[X|Y])$$ "Average of the variances plus variance of the averages."
Independence recap
If $X\perp Y$: $p_{X|Y}(x|y)=p_X(x)$, $f_{X|Y}(x|y)=f_X(x)$, $E[X|Y]=E[X]$.

Uniform on a Region
$(X,Y)\sim\mathrm{Unif}(R)$, area $A$: $$f(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{A}\,\mathbf{1}_{(x,y)\in R}$$ $P((X,Y)\in B)=\mathrm{Area}(B\cap R)/A$ — pure geometric probability.
Bayes for Densities
$$f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=\dfrac{f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\,f_X(x)}{f_Y(y)}$$ $f_Y(y)=\int f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\,f_X(x)\,dx$ (total prob).

Change of Variables (§6.6)

Univariate (monotone $g$)
$$f_Y(y)=f_X(g^{-1}(y))\left|\dfrac{d}{dy}g^{-1}(y)\right|$$
Bivariate Jacobian
Given $U=g_1(X,Y)$, $V=g_2(X,Y)$, solve for $X=h_1(U,V)$, $Y=h_2(U,V)$. $$J=\det\!\begin{pmatrix} \partial x/\partial u & \partial x/\partial v \\ \partial y/\partial u & \partial y/\partial v \end{pmatrix}$$ $$f_{U,V}(u,v)=f_{X,Y}(h_1,h_2)\,|J|$$ (Or equivalently divide by $|J_{X\to UV}|$ — same number, whichever you compute.)
4-Step Recipe
  1. Write $U=g_1, V=g_2$.
  2. Solve for $X,Y$ in terms of $U,V$.
  3. Compute $|J|=\left|\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial u}\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial v}-\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial v}\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial u}\right|$.
  4. Find new support (image of original region).

Order Statistics

Joint density of all order stats
$$f_{X_{(1)},\dots,X_{(n)}}(x_1,\dots,x_n)=n!\prod_i f(x_i)$$ on $x_1<x_2<\dots<x_n$.
$k$-th order stat
$$f_{X_{(k)}}(x)=\dfrac{n!}{(k{-}1)!(n{-}k)!}F(x)^{k-1}(1{-}F(x))^{n-k}f(x)$$ $X_{(1)}=\min$, $X_{(n)}=\max$.
CDF Method (alt to Jacobian)
For a single $Z=g(X,Y)$ (no 2nd variable needed):
  1. $F_Z(z)=P(g(X,Y)\le z)=\displaystyle\iint_{g(x,y)\le z}f(x,y)\,dx\,dy$.
  2. Differentiate: $f_Z(z)=F_Z'(z)$.
Use when Jacobian is messy or dimension reduces ($2\to 1$).
Min / Max of iid
$X_1,\dots,X_n$ iid with CDF $F$:
$F_{\max}(t)=F(t)^n$, $F_{\min}(t)=1{-}(1{-}F(t))^n$
Exp race: $X_i\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda_i)$ indep:
  • $\min X_i\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\sum\lambda_i)$
  • $P(X_j{=}\min)=\lambda_j/\sum\lambda_i$
  • $\min$ and which-is-min are independent
  • $P(X_1<X_2)=\lambda_1/(\lambda_1{+}\lambda_2)$

Standard Normal $\Phi$

Notation & symmetry
$Z\sim N(0,1)$: $\Phi(z)=P(Z\le z)=\int_{-\infty}^z\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-t^2/2}dt$
Symmetry: $\Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z)$
$P(a<Z<b)=\Phi(b)-\Phi(a)$
$P(\lvert Z\rvert>z)=2(1-\Phi(z))$
Standardize: if $X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, then $Z=\dfrac{X-\mu}{\sigma}\sim N(0,1)$

Exam Playbook

Joint density on region (Q1-style)
  1. Sketch support → set integration bounds.
  2. Normalize: $\iint f = 1$ → solve for constant.
  3. Marginal: integrate out the other variable.
  4. Probability: integrate $f$ over the event region.
  5. Independence: check if support is a rectangle AND $f$ factors.
Jacobian (Q2-style)
  1. Pick $U,V$ that simplify the geometry.
  2. Invert to $X(U,V), Y(U,V)$.
  3. Compute $|J|$.
  4. Map support.
  5. Write $f_{U,V}$ and integrate.
Discrete triangular (Q3-style)
  1. Find $C$ from $\sum\sum p(n,m)=1$.
  2. Use $k=n{-}m$ or similar substitution to simplify sums.
  3. Recall $\sum_{n=1}^N n=\tfrac{N(N+1)}{2}$, $\sum n^2=\tfrac{N(N+1)(2N+1)}{6}$.
  4. Conditional: $P(A|B)=P(A{\cap}B)/P(B)$.
CDF→E[X] (Q4-style)
  1. Differentiate $F$ to get $f$.
  2. Recognize $f$ as known (Exp, Γ, Rayleigh, …).
  3. $E[X]=\int x f(x)\,dx$. Substitution $u=x^2$ for Rayleigh-ish.
  4. Reduce to Gamma: $\int_0^\infty u^{s-1}e^{-u}du=\Gamma(s)$.
Traps checklist
  • ☐ Is support rectangular?
  • ☐ Did I take $|J|$ not $J$?
  • ☐ Did I update the support after transformation?
  • ☐ Did I square the density-variable change for CDF/PDF conversion?
  • ☐ Is the denominator in conditional $> 0$?
Convolution Checklist
$f_{X+Y}(t)=\int f_X(x)\,f_Y(t-x)\,dx$:
  1. Write $f_X(x)\cdot f_Y(t-x)$.
  2. Find where BOTH are nonzero (region depends on $t$).
  3. Integrate $x$ over the valid range.
  4. Simplify — recognize known density type.
Shortcut: use famous sums table if distributions match.
Conditional Density Steps
  1. Marginal: $f_Y(y)=\int f(x,y)\,dx$.
  2. Divide: $f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=f(x,y)/f_Y(y)$.
  3. Verify $\int f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)\,dx=1$ for fixed $y$.
  4. Recognize — read off $E[X\mid Y=y]$ by distribution.
  5. Tower: $E[X]=\int E[X\mid Y=y]\,f_Y(y)\,dy$.
Useful Sums / Series
Geometric (finite): $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}r^k=\dfrac{1{-}r^N}{1{-}r}$
Geometric (infinite, $|r|<1$): $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^\infty r^k=\dfrac{1}{1{-}r}$
$\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^\infty k\,r^{k-1}=\dfrac{1}{(1{-}r)^2}$
$\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^\infty k^2\,r^{k-1}=\dfrac{1{+}r}{(1{-}r)^3}$
Exp series: $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^\infty \dfrac{\lambda^k}{k!}=e^\lambda$
$\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^N k=\tfrac{N(N{+}1)}{2}$, $\displaystyle\sum k^2=\tfrac{N(N{+}1)(2N{+}1)}{6}$
Normalizing Constant
Given $f(x,y)=C\,g(x,y)$ on region $R$: $$C=\dfrac{1}{\iint_R g(x,y)\,dx\,dy}$$ Always do this FIRST on any joint-density problem.
