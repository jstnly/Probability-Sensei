Joint & Independence (6aa–6bb)

Joint CDF
Chance both are at or below their threshold.
$F(x,y)=P(X\le x,\ Y\le y)$
$F$ = joint CDF · $X,Y$ = two random variables · $x,y$ = threshold numbers
Joint PMF (discrete)
Chance X equals exactly $x$ AND Y equals exactly $y$.
$p(x,y)=P(X=x,\ Y=y)$
Marginal of X: add over all $y$ → $p_X(x)=\sum_y p(x,y)$
$p$ = joint PMF · $p_X$ = marginal PMF of X alone
Joint PDF (continuous)
Density whose double integral over a region gives the chance of landing in it.
$P((X,Y)\in A)=\iint_A f(x,y)\,dx\,dy$
Marginal of X: integrate out $y$ → $f_X(x)=\int f(x,y)\,dy$
From CDF: $f(x,y)=\dfrac{\partial^2 F}{\partial x\,\partial y}$
$f$ = joint PDF · $A$ = region in the plane
Normalizing constant — DO THIS FIRST
Find $k$ so total probability is 1.
$\iint_R k\,g(x,y)\,dx\,dy=1\ \Rightarrow\ k=\dfrac{1}{\iint_R g}$
$k$ = unknown constant · $R$ = region where $f$ is nonzero · $g$ = shape of density before scaling
Average of a function of X,Y
$E[g(X,Y)]=\iint g(x,y)\,f(x,y)\,dx\,dy$ (cts)
$E[g(X,Y)]=\sum_x\sum_y g(x,y)\,p(x,y)$ (disc)
$g$ = any function you want the average of
Independence (6bb)
X and Y are independent when the joint factors into marginals.
$F(x,y)=F_X(x)\,F_Y(y)$ (or PDF/PMF)
Both must hold: (1) $f$ factors as $g(x)\,h(y)$ AND (2) region is a rectangle.
"$\perp$" means independent
Non-rectangular trap (6bb)
If the region is NOT a rectangle, X and Y are NOT independent — even when the formula looks like a product.
Example: $f(x,y)=2$ on $0<x<y<1$ is a triangle. The constraint $x<y$ ties them.
Sketch the support
Draw the boundary lines, pick a test point, shade.
  • $\{0<x<y<1\}$: upper triangle of unit square (above diagonal)
  • $\{x{+}y<1,\ x,y>0\}$: triangle with corners $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$
  • $\{x^2{+}y^2<r^2\}$: disk of radius $r$ (go polar)
Double-integral bounds (6rr)
Sketch → pick outer variable → inner limits depend on outer.
RegionOrder $dy\,dx$
$0{<}x{<}y{<}1$$\int_0^1\!\int_x^1 f\,dy\,dx$
$x{+}y{<}1,\,x,y>0$$\int_0^1\!\int_0^{1-x} f\,dy\,dx$
Fubini: when $f\ge 0$, you can swap $dx\,dy \leftrightarrow dy\,dx$ — just redo the limits.
Exp race (6aa)
If $X\sim\mathrm{Exp}(a)$ and $Y\sim\mathrm{Exp}(b)$ are independent:
$P(X<Y)=\dfrac{a}{a+b}$
$a,b$ = rate parameters. The faster rate (bigger) is more likely to finish first.

Sums of Independent RVs (6cc–6dd)

What convolution is
The distribution of the sum $X+Y$ when X and Y are independent.
Discrete: $p_{X+Y}(a)=\sum_k p_X(k)\,p_Y(a{-}k)$
Continuous: $f_{X+Y}(a)=\int f_X(x)\,f_Y(a{-}x)\,dx$
Only integrate/sum where BOTH $f_X$ and $f_Y$ are nonzero.

Variances always add (6dd)
For independent X and Y:
$E[X+Y]=E[X]+E[Y]$ (always true)
$\mathrm{Var}(X+Y)=\mathrm{Var}(X)+\mathrm{Var}(Y)$ (needs independence)
Linear combo: $\mathrm{Var}(aX+bY)=a^2\mathrm{Var}(X)+b^2\mathrm{Var}(Y)$
Normal + Normal (6dd)
Sum of independent normals is normal with means summed and variances summed:
$\sum X_k\sim N\!\left(\sum\mu_k,\ \sum\sigma_k^2\right)$
Linear combo: $aX+bY\sim N(a\mu_X+b\mu_Y,\ a^2\sigma_X^2+b^2\sigma_Y^2)$
Proof by convolution + "completing the square" inside the exponent.
iid Exp → Gamma (6dd)
Sum of $n$ independent Exp$(\lambda)$ random variables.
$Y_n=X_1+\cdots+X_n\sim\Gamma(n,\lambda)$
$f_{Y_n}(t)=\dfrac{\lambda^n\,t^{n-1}\,e^{-\lambda t}}{(n-1)!},\ t\ge 0$
Proved by induction: each new Exp convolves to bump the shape up by 1.
Beta integral & Gamma+Gamma (6dd)
Key integral that makes Gamma+Gamma work:
$\int_0^1 x^{a-1}(1{-}x)^{b-1}\,dx=\dfrac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}$
Scaled: $\int_0^t x^{\alpha-1}(t{-}x)^{\beta-1}\,dx=\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}\,t^{\alpha+\beta-1}$
Gamma function: $\Gamma(\alpha)=\int_0^\infty x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}\,dx$
Beta distribution: $X\sim\mathrm{Beta}(a,b)$, $E[X]=\dfrac{a}{a+b}$

Conditionals (6ee–6ff)

What "conditional" means
Once you know the value of Y, the distribution of X narrows. "Conditional on Y=y" means pretend you already observed Y=y.
Conditional PMF (6ee)
Chance X equals $x$ GIVEN Y already equals $y$.
$p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=\dfrac{p(x,y)}{p_Y(y)}$ when $p_Y(y)>0$
For fixed $y$: $\sum_x p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=1$
Conditional PDF (6ff)
Density of X given Y=y. It's a shorthand for the chance that X is in a tiny window around $x$ given Y=y.
$f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=\dfrac{f(x,y)}{f_Y(y)}$ when $f_Y(y)>0$
For fixed $y$: $\int f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)\,dx=1$
Conditional expectation
Average of X once you've locked in Y=y.
$E[X\mid Y=y]=\sum_x x\,p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)$ (disc)
$E[X\mid Y=y]=\int x\,f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)\,dx$ (cts)
$E[X\mid Y]$ (without a value) is itself a random variable — a function of Y.
Law of total expectation (tower)
To find $E[X]$ when direct is hard, average the conditional over all $y$.
$E[X]=E\big[E[X\mid Y]\big]$
Cts: $E[X]=\int E[X\mid Y{=}y]\,f_Y(y)\,dy$
Disc: $E[X]=\sum_y E[X\mid Y{=}y]\,p_Y(y)$
Independence ⇒ conditional = marginal (6ee)
If $X\perp Y$: knowing Y tells you nothing about X.
$p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=p_X(x)$,  $f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=f_X(x)$,  $E[X\mid Y]=E[X]$
Uniform on a region
$(X,Y)$ uniform on region $R$ with area $A$:
$f(x,y)=\dfrac{1}{A}$ for $(x,y)\in R$, else $0$
$P((X,Y)\in B)=\dfrac{\text{Area}(B\cap R)}{A}$ — pure geometry.
Recognize density by form
Strip constants, match the shape, then normalize.

Change of Variables (6gg)

What the Jacobian does
When you swap from $(X,Y)$ to a new pair $(U,V)$, area elements stretch. The Jacobian $|J|$ is the stretch factor that keeps total probability equal to 1.
Univariate transform
If $Y=g(X)$ with $g$ smooth and one-to-one:
$f_Y(y)=f_X(g^{-1}(y))\left|\dfrac{d}{dy}g^{-1}(y)\right|$
$g^{-1}$ = inverse function · $|\cdot|$ = absolute value (never negative density)
Bivariate Jacobian (6gg)
Set $U=g_1(X,Y)$, $V=g_2(X,Y)$. Solve for $X=h_1(U,V)$, $Y=h_2(U,V)$.
$J=\det\!\begin{pmatrix} \partial x/\partial u & \partial x/\partial v \\ \partial y/\partial u & \partial y/\partial v \end{pmatrix}=\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial u}\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial v}-\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial v}\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial u}$
$f_{U,V}(u,v)=f_{X,Y}(h_1,h_2)\,|J|$
4-step recipe
  1. Write $U,V$ as functions of $X,Y$.
  2. Solve for $X,Y$ in terms of $U,V$.
  3. Compute $|J|$ (always take absolute value).
  4. Find the new region — map old boundaries into $(U,V)$ plane.
Common substitutions

$U{=}X{+}Y,\ V{=}X$$X{=}V,\ Y{=}U{-}V$$1$

Polar (6gg + 6rr)
$X=R\cos\Theta$, $Y=R\sin\Theta$ → $|J|=R$, so $dx\,dy=r\,dr\,d\theta$.
Example (standard normals): if $X,Y\sim N(0,1)$ indep, then
$f_{R,\Theta}(r,\theta)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\,r\,e^{-r^2/2}$ on $r\ge 0,\ 0\le\theta<2\pi$
$R$ = radius · $\Theta$ = angle

Gamma-function identities
  • $\Gamma(n)=(n{-}1)!$ when $n$ is a positive integer
  • $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}$
  • $\Gamma(x{+}1)=x\,\Gamma(x)$ (recurrence)
Memoryless (Exp)
An Exp "forgets" how long you've already waited.
$P(X>s{+}t\mid X>s)=P(X>t)$
$s$ = time already waited · $t$ = extra time

Exam Playbook

Q1 — Joint density on a region
  1. Sketch the region. Pick outer variable.
  2. Normalize: $\iint f=1$ → solve for the constant.
  3. Marginals: integrate out the other variable.
  4. $P(\text{event})$: integrate $f$ over the event region.
  5. Independence: check BOTH — $f$ factors AND region is rectangle.
Q2 — Jacobian transform
  1. Pick $U,V$ that simplify the geometry.
  2. Solve for $X(U,V),\ Y(U,V)$.
  3. Compute $|J|$.
  4. Map the region — find the new bounds in $(U,V)$.
  5. Write $f_{U,V}=f_{X,Y}(h_1,h_2)\cdot|J|$ and integrate.
Q3 — Discrete conditional
  1. Find $C$ from $\sum\sum p(n,m)=1$.
  2. Marginal: $p_Y(y)=\sum_x p(x,y)$.
  3. Conditional: $p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=p(x,y)/p_Y(y)$.
  4. $E[X\mid Y{=}y]=\sum_x x\,p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)$.
Q4 — CDF → PDF → E[X]
  1. Differentiate $F$ to get $f$: $f(x)=F'(x)$.
  2. Match $f$ to a known form (Exp, Γ, etc.).
  3. $E[X]=\int x\,f(x)\,dx$.
  4. Reduce to Gamma: $\int_0^\infty u^{s-1}e^{-u}\,du=\Gamma(s)$.
Convolution checklist
For $f_{X+Y}(t)=\int f_X(x)\,f_Y(t{-}x)\,dx$:
  1. Write the product $f_X(x)\cdot f_Y(t{-}x)$.
  2. Find the $x$-range where BOTH are nonzero (depends on $t$).
  3. Integrate over that range.
  4. Recognize a known density — done.
Conditional density checklist
  1. Marginal: $f_Y(y)=\int f(x,y)\,dx$.
  2. Divide: $f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=f(x,y)/f_Y(y)$.
  3. Verify: $\int f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)\,dx=1$ for each fixed $y$.
  4. $E[X\mid Y{=}y]=\int x\,f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)\,dx$.
Normalizing constant
Always the first move on a "find $k$" problem.
$f(x,y)=C\,g(x,y)$ on region $R$:
$C=\dfrac{1}{\iint_R g(x,y)\,dx\,dy}$
Traps checklist
  • ☐ Is the support a rectangle? (if no → not independent)
  • ☐ Did I use $|J|$, not just $J$?
  • ☐ Did I update the region after transforming?
  • ☐ Is the denominator in a conditional $>0$?
  • ☐ Did I normalize first?
